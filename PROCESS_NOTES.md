# Process notes

## Stage 1: seeded data generator
- Built `scripts/generate-data.ts` (mulberry32, seed 20261020, run with `npm run generate-data`) → 14 table JSONs + `meta.json` in `src/data/` (~1.5 MB). It generates random history (1,246 Asia enquiries / 523 deals; 441 / 223 for Rotterdam), then tunes S1 (DPD ≈ 4, claim share exactly 3× the desk, exposure $3.92M + $0.40M, average premium $10) and asserts every S1–S4 figure, so a seed change can't silently break the demo. Two runs produce byte-identical output.
- Dead end: the first pass gave 4 customers credit limits of $13–20M (heavy traders on 45-day terms in both BUs). Fixed by dropping 45-day terms, narrowing the activity spread, capping limits at $10M, and seeding today's generic queue only from customers under $6M exposure, so S1 stays the only credit counter. Known quirk: the lowest lost premium on S1's similar deals is $3 (random loss at the floor). It's left as generated.
- Assumptions: the demo clock is frozen at `2026-10-20T09:00+08:00` (`meta.now`); one delivery per barge per day; declined enquiries have `outcome = lost`; status is `open/quoted/countered/declined`; exposure = unpaid invoices + undelivered deal value (qty × price). Rotterdam's port is the fictional "North Sea Hub" (no real port names). Fake IMOs use a deliberately invalid check digit. `agg_customer_exposure` has today's snapshot only.

## Stage 2: semantic layer
- Added `src/semantic/`: `metrics.ts` is a typed registry of 40 entries, each with definition, formula, owner, certification, source tables, scope and roles. 30 are single numbers, statuses or flags; 10 are governed datasets (queue, barge timeline, win range, suggested move, the AI "Ask" series). `permissions.ts` builds the user's row- and object-level scoped view. `query.ts` is the only read path, returning `{ value, metricId, asOf, restricted? }`. Only `permissions.ts` imports the raw `warehouse.ts`, so the "same definitions, same permissions" claim holds in the code, not only in the UI.
- Verified with a throwaway script (bundled with `vite build --ssr`, not committed) that drives the demo script through `query()`: S1 COUNTER 1,100 mt at $13 and −$247k headroom; S2 COUNTER to 26 Oct on Corvana Gannet; S3 BLOCKED last; S4 QUOTE at $13.50; exact Rotterdam refusal wording. Queue mix: 33 QUOTE, 4 COUNTER, 2 DECLINE, 1 BLOCKED. One plain enquiry (margin DECLINE) sits between S1 and S2.
- Changed the plan: metrics call shared helper functions rather than calling `query()` recursively; that avoided a circular type and keeps one permission check per request. Refusals come from the permission layer (an explicit `businessUnit` param, or a row outside scope), not from AI templates, so the AI can't word around them.
- Assumptions: the barging estimate for an open enquiry is the desk's 12-month average barging cost per mt; "12 months" filters deals on delivery date and includes contracted, undelivered deals; max quantity rounds down to 50 mt; the shorter-terms counter option is fixed at 15 days; the clock is `meta.now`. Trader status changes (stage 5) aren't modelled yet.

## Stage 3: queue + enquiry layout (static panels)
- Replaced the Vite template with the two-pane cockpit: a ranked queue (time left, red under 1h; expected value; Credit / No barge / Compliance badges; blocked last) and the enquiry screen. That screen has a header with a quote-due countdown and three panels. Safe? holds KYC/screening and headroom with an expandable breakdown where other BUs show "detail restricted". Deliverable? holds an SVG barge × day timeline with the window band, ETA line and free days, plus stock. Price? holds benchmark ± delta, cost, customer vs desk premium and claims, the win-range band with n, and the suggested premium with its +$2/+$1 chips. Every figure is rendered by one `MetricValue` component straight from a `query()` result. AI brief, Ask box and actions are labelled placeholders.
- What didn't work: (1) drawing booked slots at their real hours made them thin slivers next to full-day "free" cells, so the S2 gap didn't read. Since availability is per day, a booked day is now a full grey cell, with exact times in the tooltip. (2) S2 showed a $1.55/mt expected margin, below the $3 decline line: HSFO cost sits above benchmark and the seeded customer premium was low. I raised that customer's base premium in the generator and added an S2 margin assertion. That regenerated the data; S1, S3 and S4 figures and the queue order are unchanged (re-verified). (3) Long price labels wrapped at 1280px; shortened them.
- Checked in the browser at 1440 and 1280 (no horizontal scroll, panels 290px at 1280): S1 shows −$247k, $3.92M own BU, $400k other BUs "detail restricted", 4.0 days past due, 3.0× claims and $13 = $10.02 + $2 + $1; S2 timeline shows the gap; S3 shows FLAG, no price, actions disabled. No console errors. The data is bundled into the JS (1.8 MB raw, 214 KB gzipped), which triggers Vite's 500 kB chunk warning; I left it since load stays well under 3s.
- Assumptions: the first queue item (S4) is selected on load; blocked enquiries show "—" for expected value and no customer pricing; the page has a 1280px minimum width (desktop-only, per brief).
- Layout revision after review ("too much empty space, everything blends in, header row unstructured"). Each panel is now titled by the question it answers ("Can we trade?", "Can we deliver?", "What price?"), with a subtitle and a verdict pill + coloured top edge from governed values: Over credit limit / Blocked: compliance / Clear to trade; No barge in window / Barge available; Suggest $13.00 / No price. Panels stretch to equal height; credit keeps limit/exposure/headroom visible with the breakdown still expandable; the suggested premium moved to the top of the Price panel as its answer. The enquiry header became customer as title + vessel line + a labelled fact strip (grade, quantity, window, ETA, port) + a separate quote-due block. First attempt put subtitles beside the pills and they wrapped at 1280px; moved them to their own line.
